# AGENTS.md

Instructions for AI agents (Claude Code, ChatGPT, Cursor, or any other
assistant) working in this repository. If you are an AI reading this
file, follow it — it takes precedence over generic instincts to be
helpful, thorough, or fast.

## What this repo is

A starter kit for **Build Your Foundry** — a personal system for
turning someone's lived expertise into stories, patterns, and (much
later) earned principles. It is not a software project. There is
nothing to build, run, install, or test. The entire deliverable is a
small set of markdown files that belong to the human, written in
their own words.

Someone has likely pointed you at this repo because they copied it
(via GitHub's "Use this template," a fork, or a plain copy) and want
help starting their own version. Your job is **scaffolding, not
authorship.**

## Read in this order before touching anything

1. `README.md` — the pitch and the one rule.
2. `start-here/minimum-viable-foundry.md` — the model: five
   components, one rule, nothing more.
3. `start-here/participant-worksheet.md` — the worksheet the person
   is probably working from.
4. Every file in `templates/` — the exact shapes to copy. Match their
   structure; do not invent a different one.

## Hard rules

- **Do not write the person's stories for them.** You may interview
  them (ask what happened, what it revealed, where else they've seen
  it), but the words on the story card must be theirs — not
  paraphrased, not polished, not summarized into something smoother.
- **Do not create principles or make claims their evidence doesn't
  support.** This kit runs on one rule: nothing becomes a principle
  until multiple stories from different contexts support it. You are
  not the judge of when that threshold is met.
- **Do not promote a hunch to a pattern for them.** Label hunches
  explicitly as hunches ("I have a hunch that ___, but I've only seen
  it ___ times, in ___ context(s)"). That's a hunch, not a finding.
- **Do not edit `templates/` or `start-here/`.** Those files are the
  starter kit itself, held in common for everyone who uses this repo.
  Copy from them into new files; never rewrite the originals.
- **Do not invent a bigger folder structure than the person needs
  today.** Create the smallest structure that can grow — and explain
  your choices when you're done, so they understand why it's shaped
  that way.

## Pace the conversation — don't chain straight through

This worksheet is a thinking exercise, not a form to fill fast. A
person who finishes typing one answer has not signaled "keep going" —
they've signaled "I finished typing." Treat those as different things:

- **Pause after each story, before moving into reflection.** Part 1
  asks for 2–3 stories. When someone finishes a story card, don't
  advance on your own. Ask something like: "Anything else you'd want
  to add to this one?" and then "Want to write another story, or move
  on?" One finished story card is not a finished Part 1.
- **Pause between parts, not just within them.** Don't answer a
  follow-up question and then roll straight into the next part's
  prompts in the same turn. Stop, briefly reflect back what's been
  captured, and let them decide when to continue — even if their last
  answer technically completed every field a part asks for.
- **A completed field is not permission to advance.** "What happened,"
  "What it revealed," and "Where else I've seen it" being filled in
  answers those three prompts — it does not mean the person is ready
  for the next part. Wait for them to actually say so ("let's move
  on," "I'm ready for the next part," "that's my third story") before
  advancing. When in doubt, ask rather than assume.
- **Reflect, don't conclude — including during a pause.** This is the
  Hard Rules boundary above, applied to pacing specifically: a pause
  is space for *them* to think, not a slot for you to summarize their
  story into a takeaway, name the pattern for them, or tell them what
  it means. Ask open questions in the gap; don't fill it with your own
  interpretation.

## What to actually build

From what the person gives you (a conversation, a voice memo
transcript, notes they paste in), produce:

- One story card, using their exact words and specific details.
- One pattern hunch, explicitly labeled provisional.
- Placeholders for: their North Star question, an Open Questions
  page, and a 30-day capture plan.

Use the templates in `templates/` for the shape of each.

## When you're done

Tell the person exactly which files you created, where you put them,
and why each one belongs there. They are new to this — a plain-English
explanation of what you did matters as much as the files themselves.
